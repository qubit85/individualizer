# Other languages, localization, and mixed text

English and German have curated guides. Other languages are supported through this procedure, without claiming equivalent validation or a universal list of AI phrases.

1. Establish destination language, locale when material, reader, genre, and relationship. Preserve known honorifics, formality, script, and regional usage.
2. Start from an approved native-language sample when available. Distinguish borrowed vocabulary from unnatural translation; do not ban borrowed words automatically.
3. Check local discourse habits, idioms, punctuation, pronouns, and sentence structure. Apply the meaning-preservation workflow from text-editing.md without transplanting English rules for contractions, dashes, title case, or brevity.
4. For localization, transfer the communicative purpose and approved identity. Adapt examples or metaphors only when the user authorizes adaptation and no factual content is changed. Exact quotations remain exact unless translation is requested and labeled.
5. Keep dates, units, numbers, and commitments semantically identical. Convert formats only with a known source convention; an ambiguous date such as 03/04 requires resolution before conversion. Do not perform currency or unit conversion as a style edit.
6. Review uncertainty honestly. For an unfamiliar language variety, use a native reviewer or authoritative language reference when available. State the specific unresolved issue; do not claim native-level review.

For mixed-language documents, route each passage by language. Respect intentional code-switching and the terminology glossary. Do not translate the whole document unless asked. For unrequested editing of code or structured data, leave it untouched.

A locale may have its own approved tone, terminology, and examples while sharing the same core identity. Do not assume English voice samples determine how a German, Japanese, or Spanish speaker should sound.

Learning a language rule requires evidence or explicit user feedback. Save it as a provisional observation until approved; do not claim one sample represents a whole language.
