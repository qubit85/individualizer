# Adapt identity to the medium

Read only the section needed. Use the host's available document, slide, image, or chart tool for production. Pass a compact brief containing selected profile/version, language, audience, approved constraints, factual input, and relevant tokens.

## Blog, essay, or article

Keep the writer's argument, level of opinion, explanatory depth, and approved voice. Opening and conclusion should serve this piece; avoid a universal hook or mandatory call to action. Use text-editing.md and the relevant language guide before delivery.

## Email and short messages

Preserve relationship, greeting, requested action, timing, and politeness. A concise operational message rarely needs a manifesto or a new style interview. “Human” does not mean familiar; retain Sie or formal address when appropriate.

## Marketing copy

Use audience, offer, supported proof, and desired action from the brief. Improve clarity and relevance; do not create savings, guarantees, urgency, testimonials, or emotional experiences. Benefit claims require support. Use persuasive structure only when the request calls for it.

## Graphics and illustrations

Provide canvas dimensions, palette, type hierarchy, imagery treatment, composition, and protected brand assets. Use precise geometry when required. Typography that an image generator cannot reliably render should be added or corrected with suitable design tools.

## Infographics and charts

Choose a chart that expresses the actual comparison or relationship. Preserve values, units, baseline meaning, sources, and uncertainty. Keep legends, labels, and accessible alternatives. A recognizable style must not distort data.

## Presentations

Apply recurring layout rules while varying composition to fit each slide's message. Use concise slide copy and separate speaker notes when useful. Preserve qualifications that affect the claim. Inspect alignment, overflow, font substitutions, and reading order after export.

## Documents and PDF

Use a consistent page grid, heading hierarchy, table/callout design, and typography. Preserve necessary detail. Inspect pagination, clipping, repeated headings, and any supported accessibility metadata. Never assume that successful export means correct layout.

## Cross-media fidelity

Check voice, visual hierarchy, motif where relevant, and terminology across the actual outputs. Plain-text artifacts need no visual tokens. New renderer constraints belong to an adaptation, not an unapproved change to the underlying identity.
