# English editorial guide

English is a detailed focus language, not a universal template for all languages. Determine locale only when spelling, audience, or conventions require it; otherwise preserve the source's consistent variant.

## Voice and register

- Match the audience's working vocabulary. Plain English can remain technical, formal, or expressive.
- Use contractions when they fit the author's register. For critical negatives or an accessibility-focused brief, spelling them out can be clearer.
- Prefer a direct actor and verb when the actor is known and relevant. Passive voice is useful when the action or affected object is the point.
- Keep idioms, humor, and personal asides only if appropriate to the voice and cultural context.
- Keep punctuation that clarifies the sentence. An em dash, semicolon, Oxford comma, or list is not inherently artificial.
- Use the publication's heading style. Do not automatically turn every heading into Title Case.

## Contextual cues

These examples invite review, not replacement on sight.

| Cue | Inspect for |
| --- | --- |
| “In today's fast-paced world”, “in an ever-evolving landscape” | An introduction with no subject-specific content |
| “delve”, “tapestry”, “pivotal”, “unlock”, “leverage” | A vague or repetitive cluster; preserve literal and technical senses |
| “seamless”, “game-changing”, “revolutionary” | Unsubstantiated promotion; retain quoted or evidenced claims appropriately |
| “It's not just X; it's Y” | A contrast invented for rhetorical force |
| “Moreover”, “Furthermore”, “Additionally” at every paragraph | Connectors substituting for an actual argument |
| “It is important to note”, “It is worth mentioning” | Framing that can be removed without losing scope |
| “In conclusion” plus a full repetition | A conclusion that adds no synthesis or action |

Avoid merely swapping one stock expression for another. Sentence length should follow the thought; no target “burstiness” or mandatory alternation.

## Original examples

Source: “It is important to note that the pilot may reduce processing time. The estimate is based on eight cases, and no production test has been completed.”

Revision: “The pilot may reduce processing time, based on an estimate from eight cases. It has not been tested in production.”

Keep the estimate, sample size, and missing test. Do not rewrite this as “The pilot saves time.”

Source: “Our revolutionary tool unlocks seamless collaboration. It supports comments on shared drafts.”

For promotional cleanup with unsubstantiated adjectives removable: “The tool supports comments on shared drafts.”

The edit is justified by the provided feature. Do not add a productivity claim.

Keep unchanged: “The estimator is robust to outliers.” Here “robust” has a technical meaning.

Keep unchanged: “The plan covers design, implementation, and maintenance.” Three real stages need no repair.

## Final check

Read the text as a member of its audience. Check referents, tense, regional spelling, terms, commitments, and remaining redundancy. Preserve individuality even when it is less slick than a generic marketing voice.
